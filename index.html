Business impact

Most of the business runs through applications.

RentSpree has other revenue streams, including agent subscriptions and landlord services. But applications carry most of the transactions and most of the revenue. Improving completion here has a direct effect on the business.

The redesign focused on helping more applicants finish and improving the information agents receive. It also created a path from the first application payment to deposits and rent after approval.

The problem

Where applications break down.

01

No clear sense of progress

A rental application rarely happens in one sitting. Applicants stop to find documents, confirm details, or come back later. The old flow didn't tell them how much was left or where they should resume.

02

User errors caught after reports were pulled

Applicants sometimes discovered a typo or outdated information only after their credit or background reports came back. By then the reports had already been run, with no clear opportunity to correct the source information.

03

Unclear when the application was actually submitted

Applicants couldn't tell when their application and reports had been sent to the agent. The experience looked finished while important review and confirmation steps still remained.

Arc one

Designed to be picked back up.

Reducing the number of steps helped, but the bigger change was giving the application a visible structure. The overview shows everything that remains, keeps completed work visible, and lets applicants edit finished sections without retracing the flow.

It also gives people a clear place to return. That matters for something usually completed over several sittings.

Moment one

  1. 01When a step is complete, its number becomes a check and an Edit action appears. The current step keeps its number so it's always clear what comes next.
  2. 02The primary action changes from Start application to Continue when someone returns.
  3. 03Edit only appears on completed sections. Correcting an answer never means walking backward through the entire flow.
  4. 04The agent receiving the application is named at the top. Applicants are sharing sensitive financial information, so they should know who will receive it.

Moment two

Keeping progress predictable.

Conditional questions open inline, keeping the application at four predictable steps. Additional adults receive their own application, while the optional under-18 count is used only for household size. This helps keep family status separate from screening.

Arc two

Catching errors before they cost money.

This came from support. Applicants were contacting us after submission because something in their credit or background report was wrong. Often, the source was information they had entered themselves: a misspelled legal name, an old address, or a transposed digit.

The flow never gave them a clear moment to check everything.

Once the mistake appeared in a report, the report had already been pulled. Field validation wouldn't catch every error, so applicants needed a full review before anything was sent to the bureau.

  1. 01Every section can be edited directly from the review page. Applicants can correct an answer while they still have control over it.
  2. 02"Check everything before it goes to Sarah. You can still edit." The description names the person and the deadline in one line.
  3. 03Nothing irreversible has happened yet. That's the whole reason this sits before the bureau call.

Arc three

Making payment make sense.

An earlier version put payment first. More people paid, but completion barely moved and refund requests climbed. We had shifted the drop-off without fixing it. Many applicants thought paying meant they were finished.

That changed the hypothesis. In 3.0, applicants review the full application before paying, then see their reports before submitting. Each step makes the next commitment clear.

  1. 01Payment appears after the application review.
  2. 02The page explains that payment starts the reports. It does not submit the application.
  3. 03The total shows exactly what the fee includes, including the reusable package.

Hypothesis

If applicants see the full application before paying and their reports before submitting, they’ll understand what they’re buying and exactly when the application is sent.

Making abandonment measurable

3.0 launches as an A/B test. We’re watching total completion, completion after payment, and refund requests to see whether clearer expectations improve the full journey.

An AI-empowered workflow to design and build

Using Claude Code and our design system, I built the application as a working prototype instead of a series of static screens. That let me focus on the UX, content, structure, and workflow while design, product, and engineering reviewed the complete experience together.

From applicant to tenant

Clearer reports help agents and landlords screen with more confidence. Once someone is accepted, a saved payment method makes it easier to move into deposits and rent, beginning the landlord-tenant relationship.
